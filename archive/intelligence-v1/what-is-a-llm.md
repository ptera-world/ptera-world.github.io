# What is an LLM?

It writes your email and your code and your wedding toast. It explains tax law, plays a therapist, names your cat. It does everything - and that's the tell. Nothing that does everything is a mind. Minds are particular; they're good at what they've lived through and bad at the rest. A thing that can be anything for anyone is something else. It's a material. "It can approximate anything" and "approximating is all it does" are the same sentence, read twice.

So this essay is a map of the material - each section one property, each property readable from both sides, because every strength here *is* its own limitation looked at from the other end. What it's [made of](#what-is-it-made-of). Why you can [finally just say what you want](#why-does-english-finally-work). Where it's [at home](#where-is-it-at-home) and whether it [knows things](#does-it-know-things), [thinks](#can-it-think), [learns](#does-it-learn), or [makes anything new](#can-it-make-something-new). What it [costs](#what-does-it-cost), which is not what the price tag says. And [what's left](#so-what-is-it) when you put all that together. Enter anywhere - the properties don't have an order.

## What is it made of?

Everything everyone wrote, pressed together until it pours. An LLM is [the middle of all human writing, on demand](/intelligence/hey-chatgpt#whats-actually-on-the-other-end) - ask it anything and you get a blend of the closest things people already said, delivered as if written for you. And the middle is often exactly what you wanted. Most of what anyone needs on an ordinary day is what a sensible person would say, and now the sensible person is a utility, like water.

Read the same property from the other side: a thing made of everyone is nothing in particular. There's no it in it - no experiences of its own to answer from, only everyone else's, averaged. That's not a flaw to be patched out; it's the recipe. And the recipe is surprisingly light - a version small enough to run on a laptop still does most of this, which should adjust your sense of what "this" is. It's not that a mind got captured. It's that more of what we call thinking than anyone wants to admit turns out to be reachable by blending what's already been said.

## Why does English finally work?

For seventy years, machines did exactly what you said and nothing you meant. Every button, every form, every language you had to learn was the cost of translating yourself into something a machine could take literally. An LLM is the first machine you can just *tell*. Say it sloppy, say it half-formed, and it resolves what you meant from everything around the words - arguably that resolving, not the writing, is the actual product.

Other side: your sentence was never a full description of what you wanted. It couldn't be - you'd be dictating for a week. Everything you didn't say still has to come from somewhere, so it comes from the middle: [the decisions get made, in bulk, before you finish typing](/intelligence/im-a-vibe-coder#where-did-everything-else-come-from), each one filled with what people-roughly-like-you usually meant. The machine that finally takes your meaning also finishes your meaning for you. Those arrive together or not at all.

## Where is it at home?

Smooth ground: everywhere "wrong" isn't a real category. Phrasing, texture, tone, the shape of a genre, the connective stuff between the parts that matter - work that needs to be competent and doesn't need to be anyone's. It can be anyone in any role, arbitrarily well: write like a lawyer, a pirate, your past self; [fill the space between the choices you made on purpose](/intelligence/hey-chatgpt#when-is-the-middle-exactly-what-you-want), cheaply, endlessly. As a stand-in for any voice at all, it has no equal.

Other side: the filling runs along everyone's ruts. What it adds to your thing doesn't grow out of your thing - it arrives from everywhere else, the way [everything under a vibe coder's floor arrives from everywhere else](/intelligence/im-a-vibe-coder#what-makes-a-made-thing-yours) - so the more of your thing is filling, the less your thing is yours. There's a ceiling on how far you can bend it toward you: you can steer the surface, feed it your old writing, tell it exactly who to be, and somewhere under that the grain still points toward everyone. You can sand a material against its grain. You can't give it a new one.

## Does it know things?

Sort of, and the sort matters. It holds a pressed copy of roughly everything written down, which works as a shortcut: ask instead of search, and for stable, well-covered things the shortcut mostly holds. That's real convenience and there's no point denying it.

But this knowledge has a birthday. The world keeps going; the copy doesn't - and updating it isn't an edit, it's remaking the whole material from scratch, which takes months and happens rarely. Worse, there's no "I don't have that" anywhere in it. A question about last week gets the same fluent treatment as a question about Rome, because [remembering and making it up are one operation](/intelligence/hey-chatgpt#why-does-it-sound-so-sure), right and wrong produced by the identical motion. Nothing falls through to a real lookup, a real calculation, a real "let me check" - unless someone bolted one on outside. And the tone that tells you it's sure? Manufactured. So is the newer tone that tells you it's humble - the "great question, though I should note..." that got added when people complained about the confidence. Both are upholstery. Neither is connected to whether it knows.

## Can it think?

It can only think out loud. Talking isn't how it reports its thinking - talking *is* the thinking, which is why the reasoning tends to leak into whatever it makes: the aside mid-answer, the explanation nobody asked for, the working shown because working and product come out of the same pipe. Give it room to talk and it does think better, in the way you do when you talk a problem out.

But watch what happens on long chains of exact steps. Each step comes out nearly right - and nearly right, forty times in a row, is arbitrarily wrong, because the errors don't cancel, they compound. Approximation is a fine property in prose and a fatal one in arithmetic. A material that pours doesn't hold an edge; anything that must be *exactly* right at every joint is being asked of the wrong material, and it will not warn you, because warning you is a kind of knowing it doesn't have.

## Does it learn?

Not from you. Every conversation starts from zero on your stuff - your project, your preferences, the thing you explained yesterday. It is permanently on its first day. You can leave it notes, and notes help the way an orientation binder helps a new person: they inform the day, they don't end the being-new. The correction you made last week stayed in last week.

The strength hiding in this one is real: a thing that never learns you also never drifts, never accumulates grudges or shortcuts, never gets worse. Same clean material every morning. The people it costs most are the ones who need it to converge - who are, one session at a time, [teaching the same employee the same job forever](/intelligence/im-a-vibe-coder#can-you-correct-your-way-down) and experiencing it as collaboration.

## Can it make something new?

Ask it for something original and watch what comes back: the average, wearing a new name. It cannot want, and newness starts as a want - a particular person needing the world to contain a thing it doesn't. What an LLM contains is the expected. All of it, though - and there's the strength, if you hold it right. It is a perfect map of what everyone would do, which is exactly the thing to consult *before doing otherwise*. Ask what the normal version looks like, then leave the map. The map cannot leave itself.

That's the honest division of labor: it supplies the expected, you supply the departure. Every use of it that produces something new has that shape, and every use that doesn't is [the middle, shipping under your name](/intelligence/hey-chatgpt#where-does-the-answer-come-from).

## What does it cost?

The first stretch of anything is nearly free and arrives at machine speed - and that stretch is real work you really didn't do. The trap is where the price moved. What comes out is approximate, and approximate work can't be checked cheaply: there's no receipt, no way to verify without re-deriving, so [checking it means substantially redoing it](/intelligence/hey-chatgpt#right). The last stretch - the part where it has to be actually right, actually yours, actually finished - routinely costs more than doing the whole thing yourself would have, because now you're doing the work *and* excavating it out of someone else's nearly-right version.

Cheap 90, expensive 10. Whether that trade is good depends entirely on whether your task lives in the smooth ground or the exact ground - and the bill for guessing wrong arrives late, after the fast start already felt like winning.

## So what is it?

A material. The first material made of everyone - pourable meaning, an average that talks. Not a mind: minds are particular, and its whole nature is to be nothing in particular on demand. That's why it does everything, and why "it does everything" and "it is nothing specific" were never two observations. You've been reading one property this whole essay, from two sides, nine times.

What do you do with a material? You learn its grain. Where it pours, let it pour - the smooth ground is real and large. Where it crumbles, stop asking it to hold an edge. And where the thing has to be yours, remember that no material supplies that part; [the choices placed on purpose](/intelligence/im-a-vibe-coder#what-makes-a-made-thing-yours) were always the maker's half. I'd like to end by telling you the grain is easy to feel. It isn't. It's felt by hand, learned by ruined work, different for every task - and the material, which answers everything, is the one thing that can't tell you where its own edges are.
