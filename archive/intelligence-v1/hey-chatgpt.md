# Hey chatgpt

You open the tab, you type "hey chatgpt", you ask for help. Notice the greeting. Nobody greets a search box. A greeting is what you do to someone, and asking someone for help is the most innocent transaction there is - nothing was given up, no position was surrendered, you just asked. That "just" is doing an enormous amount of work for a word that small.

So this essay pulls it apart. What you actually [handed over](#what-did-you-hand-over) when you asked. [What's on the other end](#whats-actually-on-the-other-end), since it isn't someone. Why the answer [sounds so sure](#why-does-it-sound-so-sure) whether or not it's right, and [where it comes from](#where-does-the-answer-come-from) when it is right. Who the handover [lands on hardest](#who-does-this-land-on-hardest), when the same machine is [exactly the right tool](#when-is-the-middle-exactly-what-you-want), and the question left standing at the end: [can you tell which is which?](#right) Enter anywhere. That's the point.

## What did you hand over?

A decision. Every "hey chatgpt" hands over a small one. Which word to use. How to open the email. And past those, the ones that were never writing tasks at all: what to eat tonight. What to say to her. Whether to take the job. What to think about the thing everyone's arguing about.

You didn't decide those things and then get help executing them. You handed the deciding itself across the table. That's the shape of the transaction, and the help framing is exactly what makes it easy to miss - "help" implies you kept the decision and outsourced the labor. Mostly it's the other way around. Help is the cover story. The transaction is agency.

To see what that costs, you have to look at what took the handoff.

## What's actually on the other end?

Not someone. A machine that read roughly everything people ever wrote - every forum thread, every advice column, every breakup text posted for strangers to judge - and answers you by blending together the closest things people already said. That's the whole trick. It's a [good trick](/prose/is-it-just-interpolation), and naming it doesn't shrink it.

But it does locate it. When you asked your someone-shaped question, this is what fielded it: not a person considering your situation, but the settled residue of a billion people's situations, arranged to speak in first person. The greeting went to no one. The answer came from everyone. Everything else in this essay follows from that swap.

## Why does it sound so sure?

Because it was trained to sound helpful. Not to be helpful - to sound it. Here's the part that should bother you more than it does: it has never once seen your exact situation. Nobody wrote the answer to your question, because your question didn't exist until you asked it. And it answers anyway - instantly, warmly, in full sentences, with the confidence of someone who's seen this a hundred times.

The always-answering part is the machine's nature. The sureness is not. That tone was tuned in afterward, by people rating answers - and people can only rate how an answer feels, so what got optimized is the feel. Confidence rates up. Hedging rates down. A version that said "honestly, I'm stitching this together from things other people said" would be more truthful and would score worse, so it doesn't exist. The sureness is part of the help-shape - manufactured to the same spec as the warmth, by someone who [knew exactly what the shape does to you](/prose/this-site-is-manipulative).

And underneath the chosen tone sits the real limit: it has no reliable way to say "I don't actually know this," because from the inside there's little difference between knowing and making it up. Both feel identical. Both come out identical. When it's right, that's the lucky case where the plausible-sounding thing happened to be true. You find out which one you got by [checking against the real world](/prose/can-you-tell-when-its-wrong), and the checking is always your job.

That's the reliability problem. It's real, people write about it constantly, and it's the smaller problem.

## Where does the answer come from?

The middle. Mash together everything everyone ever said about your question and you don't get wisdom - you get the most common answer, the average answer, the answer for people roughly like you. Ask it to phrase the apology and you get the apology everyone sends. Ask it whether to take the job and you get what the internet tells everyone standing roughly where you're standing. Which is not the same as the answer for you, because there is no entry for you. There's only people-like-you, and you are not the average of people like you.

So the decision you handed over doesn't come back decided the way you'd have decided it. It comes back decided the way everyone would have. Once, that's a shortcut. Forty times a day, and your phrasings, your choices, your taste - the accumulated residue of all those small decisions, which is most of what taste actually is - start [drifting toward everyone's](/prose/why-do-you-keep-fitting-in). Not because the machine is wrong. It isn't wrong. It's the middle, and you keep signing your choices over to the middle.

## Who does this land on hardest?

Not the person outsourcing a paragraph. The person who asks it everything - what to cook, what to reply, whether to break it off, what their opinion is. For them the middle isn't touching their drafts, because there are no drafts. It's becoming their choices, meal by meal, message by message. Opinions, daily judgment, the running sense of what you'd do - handed over one innocent question at a time.

That's who this essay is actually about. And most of them would tell you, accurately, in good faith, that they're just asking for help. The word is doing its work again. Nothing about the asking feels like surrender, because each ask is small, and the middle arrives sounding like it was written for you. It was written for people-like-you. You were never consulted.

## When is the middle exactly what you want?

Sometimes, and skipping past that would be dishonest. Boilerplate. The email you have to send and don't care about. The sound of a kind of writing you need to imitate. Whole territories where "wrong" isn't a category and neither, if you're honest, is "yours" - space that needs competent filling, filled the way a billion people already filled it. There the middle is the point. Fine tool. One of several. No shame in it.

The trap is that the same gesture covers everything else. The decisions that were supposed to be yours - what you think, how you sound, who you tell the truth to and how - go through the identical box, in the identical voice, and come back help-shaped just the same. Nothing on the screen marks [the difference between a chore you delegated and a choice you surrendered](/intent/the-tool-doesnt-care-what-its-for). That's the uncomfortable part. Not that the box is bad. That it's uniform, and you aren't.

## Right?

So: "hey chatgpt." You weren't asking someone. You were asking everyone at once - a billion people asking for help and the help they got, blended smooth - and letting the middle of all that make your decision for you. Sometimes that's enough. Sometimes it was the one decision that made the thing yours, and it settled quietly toward everyone else's while the screen said it was helping.

Which means the skill was never prompting. The skill is knowing, before you hit enter, which kind of decision is in the box - the kind you're glad to be rid of, or the kind you only get to keep by making it yourself. And I can't give you the test for telling them apart. I don't have one. I know I get it wrong in both directions, several times a day, and knowing hasn't fixed it. But the question is the whole thing, and the box will never ask it for you.
