# i'm a vibe coder

You described what the app should do. You looked at what showed up. You said "the login page is broken, fix it." You tested it again. You did this dozens of times until it worked the way you wanted.

That's not nothing. That used to be impossible.

## The wall

There used to be a wall between having an idea for software and having software. On one side: the thing you could picture. On the other: months of learning to code, or paying someone who already had. The wall was high and most people never got past it.

That wall fell. You describe what you want and the thing appears. You test it, correct it, ship it. The idea and the software live in the same afternoon.

Anyone sneering at that has forgotten what the wall looked like from the other side.

## What happened in between

Between "the app should do this" and the working app, a lot of decisions got made. How the pieces connect to each other. What happens when something goes wrong. What gets remembered and what gets thrown away. What runs first and what waits.

Those decisions don't show up at the surface. The app works or it doesn't. The button does the right thing or it doesn't. That's what testing can see.

But those decisions got made. Not by you - you described what the app should do. The machine filled in everything else from what it's seen before. The most common way. The shape that looks like every other app.

## The corrections

"I correct it all the time." That's true. Something looks wrong, you say "fix it," the machine changes something, you look again. The loop is real and it works.

But the corrections land where you can see. The page looks wrong - you catch that. The button does the wrong thing - you catch that. The thousand decisions underneath - not about what the app *does* but what the app *is* - those don't surface as things to correct. They aren't broken. They just weren't chosen.

That's where the fix that breaks something else comes from. The bug that keeps coming back after three tries. The app that changed easily in the first week and fights back by the sixth. The underneath, pushing through.

## What makes a made thing yours

Not the parts that work. Any app can work. What makes a made thing someone's is the [choices nobody else would have made](/prose/what-is-art#is-it-about-intent-not-talent). The weird detail that exists because the maker was paying attention to something specific. The decision nobody was forced to make.

"I make all the decisions." Of the one decision offered - what the app should do - that's true. Everything underneath, the decisions that [shape what the app actually *is*](/prose/whats-the-actual-issue-with-generative-ai#the-intent-problem), got filled in. Not by the machine exactly. By nobody. Defaulting to everyone.

## The promise

The whole arrangement was built on one promise: you would never need to look underneath. Describe the surface, the machine handles the rest. That's what made the wall fall.

Both claims are true at the surface. The corrections are real. The decision was real. The question is whether the thing that makes something [yours](/prose/what-is-art) lives at the surface - where you can reach it - or underneath, where you were promised you'd never need to go.
